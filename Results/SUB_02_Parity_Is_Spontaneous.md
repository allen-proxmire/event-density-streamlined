# If the world is left-handed, it chose to be

**The claim.** A substrate whose rules do not favour a handedness cannot produce a handed transport, for any number of channels. So the handedness of the weak force cannot be written into ED's law. It has to be a broken symmetry: a choice the universe made, not a rule it follows.

**Strength:** follows from the foundation, at the transport level only.
**Sector:** substrate tests. **Paper:** [Clean Substrate Vector_Parity Spontaneous](../Papers/Clean%20Substrate%20Vector_Parity%20Spontaneous.md). **Ledger row:** 94.

## How it goes

Suppose the rules are blind to handedness. Then the commitment map is related to its own mirror image by a similarity, which forces a particular determinant to be even in the wavevector, which forces a topological winding to vanish. Zero winding means vector-like, not chiral. This holds for every channel-count, and was checked numerically for one through six. So a chiral world requires the symmetry to be broken, not assumed.

## What this leans on, stated plainly

- **Transport level only.** The step from substrate transport down to a genuine Dirac sector is open, and the paper says so. Everything here is contingent on that descent.
- Which channel-count ends up chiral is inherited from representation theory, not produced by ED. The pattern in the Standard Model matches no transport winding.
- The anomaly-freedom corollary rides on the same open descent.

## How it could be killed

A parity-clean commitment map shown to carry a nonzero winding for some channel-count, which would be a chiral coupling without breaking the symmetry. A construction that fixes which channel-count is chiral from ED's own dynamics, which would overturn the "inherited casting" verdict. Evidence that handedness is a fixed law-level property rather than a contingent choice.

## What it is not

Not a derivation of the weak force's handedness, and not a derivation of the Standard Model's matter content.
