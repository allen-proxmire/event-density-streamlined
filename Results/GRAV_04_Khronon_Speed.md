# The speed of the extra gravitational mode

**The claim.** The paper claims the extra scalar mode ED's gravity carries travels at the speed of light, where generic theories in this class allow a second, different cone.

**Strength:** Not established. The argument conflicts with the standard khronometric formula that GR-IV relies on.
**Sector:** gravity. **Paper:** [GR-III_Dynamical Rule](../Papers/GR-III_Dynamical%20Rule.md). **Ledger row:** 70.

---

## What review found

- **GR-III's route:** it gets c_s = c from the absence of a λθ² term, which means λ = 0.
- **The standard formula says otherwise.** In the khronometric parametrization GR-IV uses (§3), with β = 0 the scalar speed is c_s² = λ(2−α)/[α(2+3λ)]. With λ = 0 that gives zero, not c.
- **c_s = c needs a tuning.** It requires α = λ/(1+2λ) with λ ≠ 0, and nothing in ED supplies that tuning.
- **The cited simulation doesn't bear on the question.** It shows that a dissipative term damps a wave without changing its speed. That is true of damped waves in general and says nothing about ED's scalar in particular.

## How it goes

The paper writes the scalar's speed as c√(1+ε) and argues that only the commitment-reserve sector could make ε nonzero. It argues that sector is dissipative, so it could only damp the mode, and concludes ε = 0. That parametrization is not the standard one, and in the standard one this reasoning does not give c.

## What this leans on, stated plainly

- A non-standard parametrization of the scalar speed.
- It shares the gravity block's dependence on the band-accounting premise.

## How it could be killed

It is already contradicted by the standard formula unless the tuning α = λ/(1+2λ) is derived. Empirically: a scalar gravitational-wave polarization moving at a speed measurably different from light.

## What it is not

Not an established result about ED's scalar mode.
