# The extra gravitational mode also travels at c

**The claim.** The extra scalar mode that ED's gravity carries moves at the speed of light, not at some other speed. Generic theories in this class allow a second, different cone. ED does not.

**Strength:** follows from the foundation, at leading order.
**Sector:** gravity. **Paper:** [GR-III_Dynamical Rule](../Papers/GR-III_Dynamical%20Rule.md). **Ledger row:** 70.

## How it goes

Write the scalar's speed as c√(1+ε). A nonzero ε needs a genuine kinetic term, and the only candidate source is the commitment-reserve sector. But the arrow makes that sector dissipative: a one-way drain. A dissipative coupling shows up as damping, never as a change of speed. So ε is zero and the scalar sits on the same cone as everything else. Simulation agrees.

## What this leans on, stated plainly

- **Leading order only.** GR-III says integrating out the dissipative reserve could shift the speed by a small amount, and does not compute how much. GR-IV files that as an open number, ε′, and notes it is one calculation, not two.
- Shares the gravity block's dependence on the band-accounting premise.

## How it could be killed

A scalar gravitational-wave polarization detected at a speed measurably different from light. Or a computation showing the reserve sector does supply a genuine kinetic term after all, which would restore a second cone.

## What it is not

Not a claim about the size of the scalar mode or how easily it could be seen. The claim is only about its speed, and only at leading order.
