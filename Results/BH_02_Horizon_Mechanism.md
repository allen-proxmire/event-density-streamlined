# The horizon as a decoupling surface

**The claim.** The paper claims a black-hole horizon is where the substrate's links between inside and outside die off: a different kind of object from a geometric boundary, though in the same place.

**Strength:** Identification, not a derivation. The horizon's location is taken from general relativity, and the collapse of cross-chain transport there is asserted, not computed.
**Sector:** black holes. **Paper:** [Horizon as Decoupling Surface_Trans Planckian Resolution_Planck Mass Remnant](../Papers/Horizon%20as%20Decoupling%20Surface_Trans%20Planckian%20Resolution_Planck%20Mass%20Remnant.md). **Ledger row:** 28.

---

## What review found

- **The paper says openly that the horizon radius r_H is GR's** (§3.2). Its ED content is the statement that the cross-bandwidth Γ_cross vanishes at that surface. Γ_cross is defined but never evaluated.
- **The surface gravity κ is matched to a "P12 gradient", not computed.**
- **No practical test separates this from ordinary general relativity.** An earlier version of this page offered analogue black holes as a test. They cannot probe a Planck-scale substrate: an analogue horizon's microphysics is that of its medium. That test has been removed.

## How it goes

Define the cross-bandwidth as the rate at which V5 carries content across a sphere at radius r. The paper asserts that it collapses to zero at the horizon. Because commitment is irreversible, content committed inside cannot cross back out. On this reading the one-way character is statistical rather than geometric.

## What this leans on, stated plainly

- The horizon's position comes from general relativity.
- GR-III's rule produces a b = 0 surface whose size grows with mass, but only because its linear solution is clipped at zero. That is not a strong-field derivation of a horizon.
- The identification of κ with the P12 gradient is a matching step, not a calculation.

## How it could be killed

No practical test is identified. A substrate-level calculation showing that V5 transport does not vanish at r_H would remove the identification.

## What it is not

Not a derivation of the horizon's position, its area or its temperature. It is a claim about what a horizon *is* in the substrate.
