# The horizon as a decoupling surface

**The claim.** A black-hole horizon is the place where the links between inside and outside die off. The substrate stops carrying cross-chain content across it. This is a different kind of object from a geometric boundary, though the two sit in the same place.

**Strength:** follows from the foundation, with the horizon's location borrowed.
**Sector:** black holes. **Paper:** [Horizon as Decoupling Surface_Trans Planckian Resolution_Planck Mass Remnant](../Papers/Horizon%20as%20Decoupling%20Surface_Trans%20Planckian%20Resolution_Planck%20Mass%20Remnant.md). **Ledger row:** 28.

## How it goes

Define the cross-bandwidth as the rate at which V5 carries content across a sphere at radius r. It collapses to zero at the horizon. Because commitment is irreversible, content that has committed on the inside cannot cross back out. The one-way character is statistical, coming from the kernel dying off, rather than geometric, coming from light rays.

## What this leans on, stated plainly

- The paper does not derive where the horizon sits. It takes that from general relativity and matches to it.
- GR-III, written later, showed the gravity rule forms a horizon by itself, with its size growing in step with the mass. So part of what 039 borrows was later earned. The exact radius still is not pinned.
- The identification of κ with the P12 gradient is a matching step, not a calculation.

## How it could be killed

Detection of information-bearing Hawking radiation in a way inconsistent with V5 straddling the surface. Or analogue black-hole platforms producing Hawking-like radiation with no thermal (KMS) structure, which would break the mechanism rather than the location.

## What it is not

Not a derivation of the horizon's position, its area, or its temperature. Those are separate results. This is the claim about what a horizon *is* in the substrate.
