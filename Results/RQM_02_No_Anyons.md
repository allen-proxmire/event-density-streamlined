# No anyons in three dimensions

**The claim.** In three-dimensional space, exchanging two identical particles can only give a scalar phase of +1 or −1. Continuous exchange phases (anyons) need two dimensions.

**Strength:** Textbook result, restated in ED terms. It is correct: this is Leinaas–Myrheim (1977), with the spatial dimension assumed by P06.
**Sector:** relativistic quantum mechanics. **Paper:** [Anyon Prohibition in 3_1D](../Papers/Anyon%20Prohibition%20in%203_1D.md). **Ledger row:** 91.

## What review found

- **The mathematics is correct, and ED adds nothing to it** beyond assuming three spatial dimensions.
- **The claim was overstated.** The earlier version of this page said bosons and fermions "exhaust the options". That holds for scalar phases only. Topology does not exclude the higher-dimensional representations of the permutation group (parastatistics).
- **It is "cleanest" only because nothing ED-specific is in it.** The earlier version called this the cleanest of the sixteen, and it is, for that reason.

## How it goes

Take n identical carriers in d spatial dimensions and look at the space of their arrangements. In three dimensions the loops in that space form the permutation group, whose one-dimensional representations are ±1. In two dimensions they form the braid group instead, which allows continuous phases.

## What this leans on, stated plainly

- The assumed dimension, and standard mathematics.

## How it could be killed

Nothing here is specific to ED. Genuinely three-dimensional anyons in the bulk of a material would contradict standard physics as well.

## What it is not

Not a new prediction, and not a derivation of why space has three dimensions.
