# No anyons in three dimensions

**The claim.** In three-dimensional space, swapping two identical things can only give a plus or a minus sign. Bosons and fermions exhaust the options. Anything in between needs two dimensions.

**Strength:** follows from the foundation, using standard topology. This is the cleanest of the sixteen.
**Sector:** relativistic quantum mechanics. **Paper:** [Anyon Prohibition in 3_1D](../Papers/Anyon%20Prohibition%20in%203_1D.md). **Ledger row:** 91.

## How it goes

Take n identical carriers in d spatial dimensions and look at the space of their arrangements. In three dimensions, the loops in that space form the permutation group: a double exchange can be undone. Its one-dimensional representations allow only plus or minus one. In two dimensions the loops form the braid group instead, where a double exchange is a genuine winding that cannot be undone, and continuous phases become possible. Since ED's space is three-dimensional, only the two familiar statistics survive.

## What this leans on, stated plainly

- Nothing beyond the three primitives and the standard mathematics. No paper-specific assumption, no borrowed value, no simulation.
- The anyons seen in laboratory fractional quantum Hall systems are effectively two-dimensional. The paper treats them as consistent with this, not as a counterexample.

## How it could be killed

Detection of genuinely three-dimensional anyonic statistics, in the bulk rather than on a surface. Evidence that the substrate permits two spatial dimensions. Or a flaw in the standard topology result itself.

## What it is not

Not a new prediction: physics has held this since 1977. It is a demonstration that ED reproduces it from its own starting point rather than assuming it.
