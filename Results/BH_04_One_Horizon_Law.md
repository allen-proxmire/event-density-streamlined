# Four kinds of horizon as one object

**The claim.** The paper claims that black-hole horizons, acceleration horizons, the cosmological horizon and acoustic horizons in fluids are the same substrate object seen four ways: a surface where cross-chain content saturates and stops.

**Strength:** Identification, not a derivation. Every number is standard physics, and the paper's own audit marks the identification as a position.
**Sector:** black holes. **Paper:** [BH_Rindler_Cosmological_Acoustic Horizons as One Substrate Object](../Papers/BH_Rindler_Cosmological_Acoustic%20Horizons%20as%20One%20Substrate%20Object.md). **Ledger row:** 320.

---

## What review found

- **The shared temperature law is standard physics.** The four temperatures and surface gravities come from Hawking, Unruh, Gibbons–Hawking and Unruh's acoustic analogue, and their shared law is textbook.
- **The shared substrate mechanism is claimed, not calculated.** It is not worked out for any of the four cases.
- **The acoustic case has no testable content.** A fluid's horizon lives far above the substrate scale, so calling it "the same substrate object" predicts nothing.

## How it goes

For each of the four, the paper describes what happens to cross-chain content at the surface and says it saturates the same way. The shared temperature law is then attributed to the shared mechanism.

## What this leans on, stated plainly

- ED's content is the identification alone. Everything numerical is standard.
- Two of the four cases, cosmological and acoustic, arrive through further chains of inherited results.

## How it could be killed

There is no distinctive test. A horizon without a thermal spectrum would break the standard formula, not ED specifically.

## What it is not

Not a derivation of the Hawking–Unruh formula, and not a prediction of any of the four temperatures.
