# Why kinetic energy has a 1/2 in it

**The claim.** Under a change of velocity, the substrate's position-type content stays put while its motion-type content transforms. That asymmetry is what puts the factor of one half into kinetic energy.

**Strength:** follows from the foundation, on top of the emergent metric.
**Sector:** quantum kinematics. **Paper:** [Adjacency-Bandwidth Asymmetry from Spatial Homogeneity](../Papers/Adjacency-Bandwidth%20Asymmetry%20from%20Spatial%20Homogeneity.md). **Ledger row:** 89.

## How it goes

Split the substrate's content into adjacency (where things are) and propagation (how fast content moves). Spatial homogeneity means the first is unchanged by a boost. Transport means the second is not. The resulting mismatch in how the two pieces respond is exactly the structure that produces p²/2m rather than some other power or coefficient.

## What this leans on, stated plainly

- The paper is explicit that the full Galilean group structure is open. The primitive it uses is necessary for the asymmetry, not sufficient for the whole group.
- The paper corrected its own sourcing in September 2026: it had credited bandwidth with supplying the propagation sector, when bandwidth carries amount and transport carries rate.

## How it could be killed

Detection of non-relativistic kinetic energy with a coefficient other than 1/2m. Substrate evidence that adjacency content does transform under a boost, or that propagation content does not.

## What it is not

Not a derivation of the Galilean group, and not a derivation of mass.
