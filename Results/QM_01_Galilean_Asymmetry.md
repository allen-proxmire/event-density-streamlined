# The 1/2 in kinetic energy

**The claim.** The paper claims that under a change of velocity, the substrate's position-type content stays put while its motion-type content transforms, and that this asymmetry puts the factor of one half into kinetic energy.

**Strength:** Textbook result, restated in ED terms. The 1/2 comes from the Taylor expansion of the relativistic energy, which the paper inherits. The substrate argument restates it.
**Sector:** quantum kinematics. **Paper:** [Adjacency-Bandwidth Asymmetry from Spatial Homogeneity](../Papers/Adjacency-Bandwidth%20Asymmetry%20from%20Spatial%20Homogeneity.md). **Ledger row:** 89.

## What review found

- **The argument is circular.** Step 8 of the paper's audit imports E_kin = p²/2m from the expansion of √(p²c² + m²c⁴). The "Galilean Jacobian" steps then restate that result; they do not produce the 1/2 independently.

## How it goes

Split the substrate's content into adjacency (where things are) and propagation (how fast content moves). A boost leaves the first unchanged and shifts the second. The paper identifies this with the structure of p²/2m, whose coefficient is supplied by the inherited expansion.

## What this leans on, stated plainly

- The inherited Taylor expansion.
- The paper itself says the full Galilean group structure is open.

## How it could be killed

A kinetic-energy coefficient other than 1/2 would contradict all of physics, not ED specifically. There is no ED-specific test.

## What it is not

Not a derivation of the 1/2, the Galilean group or mass.
