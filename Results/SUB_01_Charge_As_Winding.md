# Charge as a winding number

**The claim.** A U(1) polarity angle committed single-valued around a closed loop winds a whole number of times, and that number cannot change while the loop exists. The paper reads it as the topological skeleton of electric charge.

**Strength:** Textbook result, restated in ED terms. The integer winding of a U(1) phase around a loop is standard topology. The paper adds an honest negative result about the field.
**Sector:** substrate tests. **Paper:** [Charge As Topology_Winding_Integral Gauss Law](../Papers/Charge%20As%20Topology_Winding_Integral%20Gauss%20Law.md). **Ledger row:** 93.

---

## What review found

- **The winding is an integer by construction.** π₁(U(1)) = ℤ, so for a single-valued phase the result is exact. The simulation's "machine precision" check could not have failed.
- **"Integral Gauss law" is an analogy.** The fact that the winding doesn't depend on which loop you take is the ordinary vortex winding of the XY / lattice U(1) model. Gauss's law counts the flux of a field, and here there is no field, as the paper itself says.
- **"Protected by the arrow" is an interpretation**, not a separate result.
- **What holds up is the fork, a real and honestly reported negative:** a determined 1/r² field appears only when both orientation-blindness and irreversibility are dropped, that is, only by leaving ED.

## How it goes

Ask which topological quantity the participation graph admits. The survivor is the phase accumulated around a closed loop. Once polarity is single-valued, that phase is a whole number of turns. The paper reads irreversibility as protecting the number. The same number is found around any enclosing loop.

## What this leans on, stated plainly

- **No field.** At the discrete layer there is no determined local field. The paper's fork shows one appears only by giving up both orientation-blindness and the arrow.
- **Inert winding.** It couples only weakly and does not drive the stability landscape.
- **Live loops.** The integer cannot change while its loop exists, but the loop itself is not protected. In this account charge is a property of a live loop around a thing.
- **No charge spectrum.** None is produced: no ±1, no ∓⅓.

## How it could be killed

The integer winding itself cannot fail. The ED-specific claim at risk is the negative result: obtaining a determined local 1/r² field while keeping both orientation-blindness and the arrow would overturn it.

## What it is not

Not a derivation of electric charge, its values or Coulomb's law.
